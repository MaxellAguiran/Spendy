Nordic American Tankers: The Dividend Looks Safe Today; The Fleet Cycle Does Not

A historic rate spike has transformed near-term earnings and dividend coverage, but the current share price requires unusually strong Suezmax rates to persist.

A True Rate Spike

Nordic American Tankers (NYSE: NAT) reported a dramatic first-quarter 2026 improvement. Net voyage revenue increased to $77.5 million from $37.9 million a year earlier, while net income rose to $46.3 million, or $0.22 per share, from $4.2 million, or $0.02 per share. The fleet earned a time-charter-equivalent rate of approximately $47,600 per ship per day, up from $27,490 in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Management indicated that roughly 90% of the following quarter was booked at approximately $68,000 per day, while July Suezmax spot indications reached roughly $100,000-$107,000 per day. The surge followed the near closure of the Strait of Hormuz, rerouting of tanker traffic, longer voyages, elevated insurance costs, and reduced effective vessel supply.

The rate spike is real. The investment question is whether it represents a durable regime or a temporary shock. The global Suezmax orderbook includes approximately 178 vessels, or about 28% of the trading fleet, although deliveries will be spread over several years.

The Cycle: Three Years Down, Then One Quarter Up

Nordic American's annual results show how quickly tanker economics can change. Average TCE fell from $40,522 per day in 2023 to $31,603 in 2024 and $27,681 in 2025, a 32% decline in two years. Revenue fell from $391.7 million in 2023 to $291.7 million in 2025, while EPS declined from $0.47 to $0.06.

The first quarter of 2026 is therefore a sharp rebound from a weak base rather than evidence, by itself, of a permanently higher mid-cycle. Trailing earnings multiples are distorted by the trough, while forward multiples can be distorted by the current spike. The more useful question is what TCE level the present stock price requires over a sustained period.

Reverse Valuation: What $6.45 Requires

Using an 18-vessel fleet, approximately 350 operating days per vessel, a $10,000 daily operating-cost assumption, corporate overhead, interest expense, and minimal corporate tax, the current share price implies a sustained TCE near $57,000 per ship per day when valued at the selected 5.9x peer multiple. That rate is well above the recent three-year average of approximately $33,000.

The scenario analysis demonstrates the sensitivity. A $30,000 mid-cycle TCE supports about $1.73 per share at the peer multiple, or roughly $2.05 using a premium 7.0x multiple. The 2023 average of $40,522 supports approximately $3.58. First-quarter 2026 economics support roughly $4.82. Only the approximately $68,000 second-quarter booking and the July spot spike value the shares above $6.45.

A More Generous 2026 Scenario Still Falls Short

A blended scenario gives the company full credit for the strong first half and assumes the second half normalizes to approximately $35,000 per day. That produces an estimated full-year TCE near $46,400 and EPS of roughly $0.78. Applying the 5.9x peer multiple results in a value of approximately $4.61 per share, supporting a rounded 12-month target of $4.50.

The market could justify a higher price if geopolitical disruption persists or ton-mile demand remains structurally elevated. The present valuation nevertheless requires a degree of permanence that the company's recent history does not establish.

Dividend: Covered At The Peak, Vulnerable At Mid-Cycle

Nordic American declared its 115th consecutive quarterly dividend, with the latest payment set at $0.22 per share. At the July 24 reference price, the indicated annualized yield was approximately 13.6%. The payment is well supported while fleet rates remain near current levels.

At $0.22 per quarter, annual cash dividends total approximately $186 million. The author's mid-cycle model at a $30,000 TCE produces roughly $62 million of net income, implying only about one-third earnings coverage. Nordic American has varied the dividend throughout its history and has relied on financing, asset sales, or balance-sheet resources during weak periods. Investors are buying a distribution linked to spot-market economics, not a fixed-income substitute.

Balance Sheet And Fleet Renewal

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the Hold case. At March 31, 2026, net debt was approximately $250.4 million, or $13.9 million per vessel, and cash had increased to $81.1 million from $45.9 million at year-end 2025.

The capital structure includes a Beal Bank facility and Ocean Yield sale-leaseback obligations. The leverage is manageable for a tanker operator, but fleet renewal will require capital. Two newbuilds scheduled for 2028 are not yet fully funded, and older vessels must eventually be sold or replaced. A stated operating cash breakeven does not capture all long-term renewal capital required to keep an aging fleet competitive and compliant.

Risks To The Cautious View

The clearest upside risk is prolonged disruption around the Strait of Hormuz. Sustained rerouting and constrained effective supply could keep rates above historical averages for longer than the model assumes.

A structural increase in ton-mile demand would also raise the normalized TCE. Longer routes for Russian and Middle Eastern crude, tighter sanctions enforcement, and changes in trade flows could create a durable rather than temporary uplift.

Finally, vessel scrapping or delivery delays could reduce the effective impact of the orderbook. If these factors keep TCE securely above $50,000, the current price becomes more defensible. The bear case is strongest if rates revert toward $30,000 while the dividend and valuation multiple compress together.

Conclusion

Nordic American is currently producing excellent earnings, and the dividend is covered under elevated spot conditions. The company also has sufficient balance-sheet flexibility to navigate the cycle. The difficulty is valuation: $6.45 requires a sustained TCE close to $57,000, far above the recent three-year average.

The combination of strong near-term cash generation and a vulnerable through-cycle valuation supports a Hold rather than a Sell. My 12-month target is $4.50, based on a generous blended 2026 rate scenario.
