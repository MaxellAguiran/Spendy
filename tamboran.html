The Resource: Large, Encouraging, And Still Unproved

Tamboran Resources (NYSE American: TBN) controls approximately 2.8 million net prospective acres in the Beetaloo depocenter and about 29.5 million gross acres across the Beetaloo and McArthur basins. The development focus is the Mid Velkerri B Shale.

The SS-6H well provides the strongest recent operating evidence. The well used an 8,635-foot horizontal section and 57 stimulation stages. Tamboran reported an average 20-day flow rate of approximately 10.3 MMcf/d, equivalent to 11.9 MMcf/d when normalized to a 10,000-foot lateral. That result supports the argument that the formation can deliver commercially relevant flow rates.

A flow test is not the same as a proved reserve or an economic development plan. Investors still need evidence on decline curves, water handling, drilling and completion costs, infrastructure requirements, realized gas prices, and repeatability across a larger well population. Tamboran's filings explicitly distinguish contingent and prospective resources from SEC-defined proved reserves. The current investment case is therefore a wager on successful conversion, not ownership of an already de-risked reserve base.

Liquidity: Funded For Now, Dilution Built Into The Path

At March 31, 2026, Tamboran reported $88.2 million of cash, up from $39.4 million at fiscal year-end. Subsequent institutional, underwritten, and retail offerings added approximately $198 million, lifting pro forma cash to roughly $286 million. Against about $45.8 million of project debt, pro forma net cash is approximately $240 million.

That liquidity materially improves the near-term runway, but it does not fund a full LNG-scale development. At an estimated annual operating and capital burn near $140 million, current liquidity covers roughly two years before considering the much larger capital requirements of long-term field development and export infrastructure.

The cost of funding is visible in the share count. The post-transaction common share count of approximately 34.86 million is substantially above the 22.4 million diluted weighted-average shares reported for the March quarter. The comparison is not perfectly like-for-like, but it illustrates the direction of travel: equity issuance and the Falcon transaction materially reduce per-share participation in the resource.

First Gas Helps, But Does Not Resolve The Valuation

Construction of the Sturt Plateau Compression Facility was approximately 86% complete at quarter-end, with first gas targeted for the second half of 2026. The associated Northern Territory gas-sales agreement covers 40 TJ per day, approximately 39 MMcf/d, under a nine-year contract with an extension option.

At an illustrative realized price of $6.50 per Mcf, the contract represents roughly $93 million of gross annual revenue before Tamboran's economic share and operating costs. Because Tamboran owns a 50% economic interest in the relevant joint venture, attributable economics would be materially lower. First gas would validate execution and provide revenue, but the near-term contract alone does not justify a market capitalization around $1.2 billion.

Valuation: $346 Per Acre Prices In Significant Success

Enterprise value per net acre is a useful first-pass framework for a pre-revenue resource company because it makes the market's implied de-risking visible. At an enterprise value near $968 million across 2.8 million net acres, Tamboran trades at approximately $346 per acre.

The draft transaction framework places pre-commercial Beetaloo acreage around $50-$200 per acre and acreage with proved reserves around $200-$1,000 per acre. Tamboran therefore trades above the high end of the pre-commercial range even though its filings report no proved reserves.

Why The Bull Case Is So Large

The upside case is mathematically powerful because a small percentage of a vast acreage position can support a large resource estimate. One illustrative scenario assumes 875 wells across 5% of the acreage, 12 Bcf of recovery per well, and roughly 10.5 Tcf of aggregate recoverable gas. At a $4 per Mcf netback and a long-dated present-value factor near 0.4, the resulting net asset value can exceed $480 per share.

The result explains the stock's appeal, but every major input is conditional: well density, recoverable volume, decline rates, netbacks, water costs, infrastructure, taxes, financing, and the future share count. A multi-decade development cannot be valued as though those variables are already settled.

Base, Bull, And Bear Cases

The base case values the acreage at $200 per acre, the high end of the selected pre-commercial range. That produces approximately $560 million of acreage value. Adding roughly $240 million of pro forma net cash yields about $800 million of equity value, or approximately $23 per share using 34.86 million shares.

A material proved-reserve booking and progress toward binding LNG offtake agreements could support a $45-$50 bull case. A further first-gas delay, weak reserve booking, lower gas prices, or additional dilution could push the shares toward the $10-$13 bear range.

Catalysts And Risks

First-gas execution is the nearest catalyst. Delivery during the second half of 2026 would demonstrate operating progress; another delay would extend cash burn and revive financing concerns.

Reserve certification is more important for valuation. A material proved-reserve booking would move the asset closer to the post-commercial category. A limited or absent booking would reinforce the argument that the market is ahead of the evidence.

Dilution remains a structural risk because the development path requires capital far beyond current liquidity. Gas-price volatility, Northern Territory regulation, legal challenges, and delays to Darwin-area infrastructure can also materially change the net asset value.

Conclusion

The Beetaloo resource is real, and SS-6H is a meaningful technical success. The stock nevertheless prices the acreage as though much of the remaining commercial work has already been completed. Investors are paying today for reserve booking, infrastructure, financing, and execution that will take years to prove.

I rate Tamboran Hold rather than Sell because the near-term catalysts are unusually binary and a strong reserve booking could close the valuation gap in the bullish direction. The 12-month base-case target is $23.
