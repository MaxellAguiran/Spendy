How the check works

Learn from earlier weeks. Check against later weeks.

The analysis learns from weeks 1–130. It and the simple last-year comparison are then checked once against the final 26 weeks.

Information used

The generated example includes weekly search, social, email, and partner spending, yearly patterns, demand that did not come from ads, effects that carry into later weeks, the point where extra spending helps less, and ordinary noise.

Fair time split

Weeks 1–130 are used to learn. Weeks 131–156 stay separate until the final check. Later weeks are never mixed into the earlier ones.

Simple comparison

The simple approach uses revenue from the same week one year earlier. Both approaches are judged by how many dollars their weekly estimates miss by.

New analysis

The new approach accounts for long-term movement, yearly patterns, effects that continue into later weeks, and the point where another dollar is estimated to help less.

Technical definitions for checking the work

For auditability: “average dollar error” is mean absolute error (MAE). The evidence file also reports root mean squared error (RMSE). The fitted method is a regularized regression with lagged channel effects, adstock, logged response features, trend, and annual seasonality. Weeks 131–156 are the chronological holdout.

Limits and reasons it could fail

What this result does not prove.

  • Generated data is cleaner and more regular than a real market.
  • A spending pattern in this example does not prove that spending caused the result.
  • The analysis says nothing about spending above or below the levels shown.
  • The suggested split assumes customer behavior and the total budget do not change suddenly.
