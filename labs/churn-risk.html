How the check works

Learn from one group. Check on a separate group.

The analysis learns from the first 4,000 customers and is checked once on another 1,000. Finding likely leavers and proving that a call would help are kept separate.

Customer histories

The generated example includes how long each customer has stayed, recent use, gaps in communication, billing changes, delivered results, support contacts, monthly revenue, whether the customer left, and how much warning appeared.

Separate final group

The first 4,000 customers are used to learn. The final 1,000 stay separate until the final check.

Simple comparison

The simple approach gives every customer the same average chance of leaving. Both approaches are checked on the same 1,000 separate customers.

What is measured

The checks ask whether predicted risks match what happened, how many likely leavers appear near the top of the call list, how much revenue is currently at risk, and how much warning the team gets.

Technical definitions for checking the work

For auditability: average probability error is the Brier score, with log loss as a second check. The reliability chart is a calibration curve. The first-100 measure is top-decile precision, and its comparison with random outreach is lift. The final 1,000 accounts are the held-out cohort.

Limits and costs

A ranked list does not keep a customer.

  • Finding likely leavers does not prove that calling them will work.
  • Revenue at risk is money currently exposed, not money saved.
  • Calling customers who would have stayed uses staff time and can annoy them.
  • The risk estimates must be checked again after important business changes.
