02 · Churn prediction

Which customers deserve attention first?

For teams that have more customers who may leave than they can contact at one time.

See the customer example built from generated data
What you decide
Which customers enter the first call list at the number your team can handle.
What I need
Customer history captured before people left, a clear definition of leaving, how many people your team can contact, how much warning you need, and the cost of contacting the wrong people.
What you get
A ranked customer list, a practical contact cutoff, likely timing, revenue currently at risk, clear limits, and repeatable steps.
When I stop
The risk estimates are no better than giving every customer the same average risk, or there is no useful action to take.
How the work runs

Four steps from question to working plan.

I do not promise an outcome. We agree the work only after the choice, business limits, and available information are clear.

  1. 01Define

    Name the choice, timing, team limit, and cost of a wrong answer.

  2. 02Check

    Check dates, missing information, accidental use of future facts, and the simple comparison.

  3. 03Test

    Build with earlier information; check once on later weeks or separate customers.

  4. 04Deliver

    Provide the working view, instructions, uncertainty, and when to check it again.

Fit

The work is useful only when someone can act on it.

A good fit

  • A choice that repeats and matters to the business.
  • Past information that existed before the result.
  • A team able to act on a spending plan or customer list.

Not a good fit

  • A request for guaranteed results.
  • No fair simple comparison or separate information for a final check.
  • A complicated analysis wanted only because data exists.

FAQ

Before the work starts.

What information do you need?

Enough past information to recreate what your team knew when each choice would have been made. The exact fields depend on the choice.

How much information is enough?

There is no single number. It depends on how often the choice comes up, how much the business has changed, how rare customer departures are, seasonal patterns, and how much information can be saved for the final check.

What if the simple comparison wins?

Use the simple method. A more complicated analysis is not useful merely because it was built.

Can you guarantee an outcome?

No. I can check how well the analysis worked on later or separate information. Real results also depend on what your team does, market changes, the offer made to customers, and other outside factors.

How do we begin?

Send a short email describing the choice, timing, available history, and business limit. This website collects no project data.
