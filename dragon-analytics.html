Start with the decision

What needs to be true before a plan is useful?

The plan should be readable by the people who run the ads, make the creative, own growth, and review the money.

One fixed total
The monthly amount that must reconcile exactly across all active setups.
Current activity
A plain picture of which ads are active and what has happened recently.
A review moment
A cadence for discussing changes before next month’s budget is committed.
An honest threshold
When the evidence is not useful enough, the right output is “no plan yet.”

The three-step loop

From a fixed amount to a defensible action list.

It is a planning conversation made compact, not a dashboard made louder.

  1. 01

    Set the constraint.

    Choose the total monthly budget and the active setups it needs to cover.

  2. 02

    Check the history.

    Review past outcomes and verify whether they support a useful next-month plan.

  3. 03

    Hand off the decisions.

    Give each setup a next action, a next amount, and a moment to review.

What lands in the meeting

A short decision pack, not vague advice.

Every setup gets a direct action: increase, keep, reduce, or cut. The proposed amounts reconcile to the fixed total. The note beside each action makes the next review conversation easier.

The guardrail

“No plan yet” is an answer you can trust.

When the available history is not strong enough for a reliable recommendation, Spendy withholds the plan. No invented certainty. No favorite result shown without its checks.

Questions people ask

What does a team need to bring?

A view of active ads, recent outcomes, the fixed budget, and the people who should be part of the review.

What does a team receive?

One action and one amount per active setup, an exact total-budget reconciliation, and a clear review cadence.

Does a budget plan promise sales?

No. A plan is a practical decision aid based on available evidence, not a promise about future performance.
