Approach

Precision first. Plain language second — and they are not in conflict.

I write plainly on purpose. Plain writing is not simplified thinking; it is what remains once you understand a problem well enough to stop hiding behind vocabulary. A model nobody can explain to the person who has to act on it is not finished work.

That matters commercially as well. Many of the people I work with use English as a second language, which has no bearing on how sharp they are about their own business. So I define a term once, keep sentences short, and drop the jargon that exists mainly to sound expensive. Everything technical stays in the work — it just doesn't get used as a screen.

The rest is method. Compare against a real baseline. Test on data the model has never seen. Report a range rather than a false decimal. Say plainly when the evidence does not support an answer, which happens more often than most consultants admit.
