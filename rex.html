REX American Resources: A Debt-Free Ethanol Operator Priced For A Cycle That May Not Last

REX delivered an exceptional first quarter, but the current valuation requires peak margins, durable 45Z benefits, and a premium multiple to persist simultaneously.

The Setup: A Genuine Earnings Inflection

REX American Resources (NYSE: REX) reported an exceptional fiscal first quarter of 2026. Net sales were $156.5 million, compared with $158.3 million a year earlier, as slightly higher gallons sold were offset by lower ethanol pricing. Gross profit more than doubled to $29.1 million from $14.3 million, lifting gross margin to 18.6% from 9.1%.

The improvement came from stronger crush economics and $7.5 million of Section 45Z clean-fuel production tax-credit income. The credit accounted for slightly more than half of the $14.7 million year-over-year increase in gross profit. Equity earnings from the four minority-owned Big River plants more than tripled to $3.6 million. Income before tax rose to $26.1 million from $13.6 million, and diluted EPS increased 115% to $0.56.

The balance sheet strengthens the result. REX ended the quarter with $117.7 million of cash, $246.6 million of short-term investments, no long-term debt, and authorization to repurchase approximately 2.36 million shares. The central question is not whether the quarter was strong; it is whether an 18.6% gross margin represents a new base or a cyclical high.

The Cycle: A Wide Range Of Outcomes

REX's five-year history demonstrates the volatility of ethanol economics. Gross margin was approximately 4.4% in fiscal 2021, 12.8% in 2022, 9.2% in 2023, 14.9% in 2024, and 15.4% in 2025. The five-year median is 12.8%, materially below the first-quarter 2026 result.

Commodity companies can look optically cheapest near peak earnings and most expensive near a trough. A low P/E at the top of a cycle is not automatically a bargain because the earnings denominator may be temporarily inflated. REX's trailing and forward multiples incorporate the first quarter of 45Z income and margins above every full-year observation in the five-year window. A through-cycle framework is therefore more useful than a single-period earnings multiple.

Valuation: What The Current Price Requires

REX owns controlling interests in One Earth Energy and NuGen Energy and minority stakes in four Big River plants. The consolidated facilities have approximately 300 million gallons of annual ethanol capacity. The valuation model applies a range of normalized operating margins and peer-relative EV/EBITDA multiples rather than extrapolating the latest quarter indefinitely.

The base case assumes a 12.4% normalized margin, including an annualized 45Z contribution, and a 7.0x multiple. That produces a fair value of approximately $33.62 per share. A similar base case without 45Z falls to $27.19. The bear case, using a 3% margin and 5.0x multiple, produces $18.71.

Only the bull case reaches the market price: a 17% margin, continued 45Z benefits, and an 8.0x multiple generate approximately $43.46 per share. At the $43.70 reference price, investors receive almost no margin of safety even if all three favorable assumptions hold.

The 45Z Credit: Material, But Finite

Section 45Z is the largest swing factor in normalized value. REX recognized approximately $7.5 million of consolidated 45Z income in the first quarter, equivalent to about $30 million on a simple annualized basis. That amount is significant relative to the company's five-year median pretax income of approximately $75.8 million.

The credit is available under current law through December 31, 2029, and the eventual run rate depends on carbon-intensity calculations, rulemaking, qualifying feedstocks, and operating performance. Treating the first-quarter amount as permanent materially raises fair value. Removing it from the base case reduces estimated value from $33.62 to $27.19, showing that roughly one-fifth of the 45Z-inclusive base value depends on a finite policy benefit.

Balance Sheet And Cash Flow: The Genuine Strength

REX's approximately $364 million of cash and short-term investments, with no long-term debt, represents roughly $11 per share of balance-sheet support. That position is substantially stronger than the leveraged balance sheets of several ethanol and agricultural-processing peers. It gives management room to repurchase shares, fund plant improvements, and absorb a weak commodity cycle without relying on expensive financing.

Cash flow remains cyclical. Operating cash flow ranged from $8.6 million in fiscal 2021 to $128.0 million in 2024. Capital spending increased sharply as REX invested in plant throughput and the One Earth carbon-capture project, causing free cash flow to move from $86.6 million in 2022 to negative $7.1 million in 2025. The five-year median free cash flow is approximately $39 million.

The clean balance sheet makes REX a resilient cyclical operator; it does not transform the company into a predictable compounder. Carbon-capture and capacity-expansion projects provide optionality, but they should not receive full value before they generate cash.

What Could Break The Cautious Case

A permanent extension or expansion of 45Z would make the policy benefit part of normalized earnings rather than a temporary bridge. A higher and more stable credit run rate could move fair value closer to $40.

A structural improvement in the corn-ethanol-gasoline spread could also raise the mid-cycle margin above the five-year median. REX is an efficient operator, so sustained favorable input and output pricing would disproportionately benefit earnings.

Commercialization of the One Earth carbon-capture project and the planned capacity expansion could create an additional 45Q or 45Z-linked earnings stream. These are credible opportunities, but the current share price already appears to capitalize a substantial portion of that upside.

Conclusion

REX has a first-rate balance sheet, real near-term earnings power, and valuable carbon-reduction optionality. The stock is not cheap on a through-cycle basis. A reasonable base case using normalized margins, a generous assumption that 45Z remains available through the modeled period, and a 7.0x multiple supports approximately $34 per share.

I would revisit the rating if REX sustains gross margins above 16% for several quarters while demonstrating that the 45Z run rate is durable. Until then, the appropriate rating is Hold with a 12-month target of $34.
