The problem

A fixed budget still creates many difficult choices.

When many setups are active, moving money from one to another is not a one-line decision. The question was simple: how should the same monthly budget be split next?

What the engagement needed

One amount and one action for every setup.

Each choice had to be clear enough to review, explain, and reconcile back to the total budget.

What was not available publicly

No client account export is shown here.

Client identity, campaign details, platform exports, commerce records, and any private working materials remain withheld.

The evidence boundary

Licensed public research, carefully distinguished from private account records.

The client engagement is real and anonymous. For this published case study, the numerical evidence comes from licensed public retail-advertising research, covering 52 historical weeks and 36 campaign setups.

The source is not a verified native client advertising or commerce export. So the result below is deliberately framed as a historical simulation, not evidence from a running campaign or a realized saving.

What this does and does not show

The result is useful context, not a promise.

  • It is a historical simulation across 12 later periods and 432 of 432 setup-period decisions.
  • The monthly budget and contribution-margin assumption are illustrative, used to make the comparison concrete.
  • It does not establish realized client savings, future performance, or a live campaign outcome.
  • The anonymous client’s identity and private records remain withheld.
